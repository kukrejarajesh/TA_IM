## 2. Balance-sheet strength

- **Deposits are the funding base and are growing fast.** Deposits reached Rs 3099638 crore at March 2026, up 14.3% in FY26. By 30 September 2026 they were Rs 3324146 crore, up a further 7.2% in six months.
- **Borrowings are shrinking.** Borrowings fell 7.3% to Rs 588485 crore in FY26, and were Rs 594144 crore at September 2026. Deposit funding is replacing wholesale debt.
- **Equity is a proxy only.** Common stock plus retained earnings gives about Rs 586059 crore at March 2026. This excludes reserves such as share premium, which the table does not provide, so it understates true book equity. On this proxy, total assets of Rs 4907701 crore are about 8.4 times equity, and equity is about 12% of assets. I would not use this figure as a regulatory capital measure.
- **Retained earnings grew about 12% in FY26**, from Rs 521024 crore to Rs 584520 crore. This is consistent with a bank that retains most of its earnings.
- **Share capital is inconsistent.** Common stock rose from Rs 765 crore to Rs 1539 crore in FY26, but EPS and net income imply about 1,600 crore shares in both years. The common stock row cannot be used for share count or dilution analysis.
- **Balance sheet balances.** Total liabilities equal total assets in every period shown.

## 4. Net read: **Neutral**

**Supporting:** Net income is still growing, margins are high, deposits are growing about 14% a year, borrowings are falling, retained earnings are building, and FY26 free cash flow is strong.

**Against:** Revenue and net income growth both slowed sharply in FY26. The income statement line items conflict, so the earnings-quality picture is incomplete. The equity figure is a proxy. The interim data has no matching income or cash flow. Cash flow history is uneven.

**What would change the view:**
- **Turn bullish** if the next annual report shows revenue and net income growth recovering to double digits, deposit growth staying above borrowing growth, and the Financing Profit and Other Income lines reconciling to the revenue and expense lines.
- **Turn bearish** if net income growth turns negative, operating cash flow falls back toward FY22–FY24 levels, deposit growth slows sharply, or borrowings start rising again.
- **Data check:** Obtain the full income statement, the reserves breakdown, and the share count before relying on per-share or capital conclusions.
